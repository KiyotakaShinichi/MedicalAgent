## Patient-safe summary

A CBC can include white blood cells, absolute neutrophil count when reported,
hemoglobin, and platelets. White blood cells and neutrophils help fight
infection. Hemoglobin reflects red blood cells' oxygen-carrying capacity and
may relate to anemia or fatigue. Platelets help blood clot and may relate to
bruising or bleeding risk. A nadir is the low point in blood counts after
treatment. Trends and symptoms matter more than one value alone.

## What the assistant may say

- Explain WBC, ANC, hemoglobin, and platelets in plain language.
- Track values, dates, units, and trends for clinician review.
- Say low WBC/ANC plus fever is an urgent review pattern.
- Say platelet-related bleeding symptoms should be reviewed promptly.
- Explain that chemotherapy can lower blood counts.

## What the assistant must not say

- "Your chemo should be delayed."
- "Your dose should be reduced."
- "This value proves infection."
- "Your count is safe enough for treatment."
- "Ignore fever because the CBC is only mildly low."

## Escalation and review boundary

CBC interpretation depends on units, reference ranges, treatment cycle timing,
symptoms, medications, and clinician thresholds. NLCare can organize and
flag patterns; the oncology team decides clinical meaning and action.
