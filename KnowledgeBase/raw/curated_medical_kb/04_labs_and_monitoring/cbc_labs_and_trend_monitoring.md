# CBC Labs and Trend Monitoring

## Purpose

This document explains CBC monitoring in patient-friendly terms. It must not be used to make treatment decisions, diagnose infection, or determine whether chemotherapy should be delayed.

## CBC overview

A complete blood count, or CBC, commonly includes white blood cells, hemoglobin, and platelets. During chemotherapy, CBC trends can help clinicians monitor treatment-related blood count changes and recovery over time.

## WBC and ANC

White blood cells help fight infection. ANC, or absolute neutrophil count, is a specific measure of neutrophils, a type of white blood cell important for infection defense. Low WBC or low ANC can increase infection risk, especially with fever or other symptoms.

## Hemoglobin

Hemoglobin is the oxygen-carrying protein in red blood cells. Low hemoglobin can be associated with anemia symptoms such as fatigue, weakness, dizziness, or shortness of breath. A care team interprets hemoglobin in context.

## Platelets

Platelets help blood clot. Low platelet counts can increase bleeding or bruising risk. Bleeding symptoms should be reviewed with the care team.

## Chemotherapy nadir

The nadir is the low point in blood counts after a chemotherapy dose. It is often discussed in relation to timing after treatment. The exact timing varies by drug, regimen, and patient.

## Baseline drop and trends

A single lab value is less informative than a trend with treatment timing. OncoTrack can compare latest values to baseline and show whether counts are falling, stable, or recovering. The assistant must not conclude that a treatment should be delayed or changed.

## Safe wording examples

- "Low WBC can relate to infection risk, especially if fever is present."
- "This value may need review by your oncology team in the context of your treatment cycle."
- "I can save the CBC values and show them on your timeline."

## Unsafe wording examples

- "Your chemo should be delayed."
- "Your dose should be reduced."
- "You definitely have an infection."
