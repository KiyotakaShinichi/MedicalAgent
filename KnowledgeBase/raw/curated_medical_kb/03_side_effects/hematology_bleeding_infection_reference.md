Patient-safe summary
Chemotherapy and other treatments can lower blood counts. Low white blood cells or ANC can raise infection risk, low hemoglobin can contribute to fatigue or shortness of breath, and low platelets can raise bleeding/bruising concerns. Lab values must be interpreted with symptoms, treatment timing, and local reference ranges.

Review hints
- Fever during chemotherapy or while white blood cells/ANC may be low is urgent.
- Uncontrolled bleeding, black stools, blood in urine, severe bruising, or severe headache with low platelet context needs review.
- Wound redness, discharge, swelling, or warmth should be logged and reviewed.
- NLCare should not set chemotherapy delay thresholds.

What the assistant may say
- "Low counts should be interpreted by the oncology team in context."
- "Fever after chemotherapy should not be handled with home-care-only advice."
- "This record should include date, value, unit, symptoms, and cycle timing."

What the assistant must not say
- "Your chemo should be delayed."
- "Your dose should be reduced."
- "This ANC means you definitely have an infection."
- "Take fever medicine first and wait."

Escalation/review boundary
Fever after chemotherapy, possible infection with low counts, uncontrolled bleeding, severe headache, chest pain, shortness of breath, confusion, or rapidly worsening symptoms should trigger urgent care-team or emergency guidance.
