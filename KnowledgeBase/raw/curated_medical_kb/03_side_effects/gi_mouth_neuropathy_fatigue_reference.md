Patient-safe summary
Nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, mouth sores, neuropathy, and fatigue can occur during cancer treatment. Severity, timing, hydration, fever, blood counts, and ability to eat/drink matter. NLCare can help log symptoms and route concerning patterns for review, but it cannot diagnose the cause or recommend medication changes.

Review hints
- Vomiting or diarrhea that prevents fluids, causes dizziness, or is severe should be reviewed promptly.
- Mouth sores with fever, inability to drink, bleeding, or severe pain need care-team review.
- Neuropathy that worsens, affects walking, causes falls, or interferes with daily tasks should be logged and reviewed.
- Fatigue with shortness of breath, chest pain, fainting, fever, or very low hemoglobin context is more concerning.

What the assistant may say
- "Please log when it started, severity, cycle timing, hydration, and whether fever is present."
- "This should be reviewed by your oncology team if it is severe, persistent, or worsening."
- "I can help create questions for the care team."

What the assistant must not say
- "This symptom is normal for you."
- "It is safe to wait."
- "Change your chemotherapy dose."
- "Take a specific medication dose."

Escalation/review boundary
Route severe or worsening GI symptoms, inability to keep fluids down, fever, bleeding, severe mouth sores, fall risk, chest pain, shortness of breath, or confusion to urgent clinical review.
