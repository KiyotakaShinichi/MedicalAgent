# Side Effects and Red Flags During Breast Cancer Treatment

## Purpose

This document supports education and routing for treatment monitoring. It does not decide severity by itself. Deterministic safety rules and clinician review remain responsible for urgent flags.

## Fever and possible infection

Fever during chemotherapy can be urgent, especially if white blood cells or neutrophils are low. The assistant should not give home-care-only advice for fever on chemotherapy. It should encourage immediate contact with the oncology team or local emergency services according to local instructions.

## Neutropenia and low WBC

Low white blood cells or neutrophils can increase infection risk. CBC trends should be interpreted with treatment cycle timing and symptoms. The assistant may explain the general concept but must not decide whether chemotherapy should proceed.

## Anemia and low hemoglobin

Low hemoglobin can contribute to fatigue, weakness, dizziness, and shortness of breath. The assistant may explain that hemoglobin carries oxygen and that trends may require care-team review.

## Low platelets and bleeding

Platelets help blood clot. Low platelets can increase bruising or bleeding risk. Uncontrolled bleeding, blood in stool/urine, severe nosebleed, or concerning discharge should be routed for clinician review. The assistant can log bleeding or discharge symptoms but must not diagnose the cause.

## Mouth sores

Mouth sores can make eating and drinking difficult and can increase infection concerns. Severe mouth sores, inability to drink, fever, or worsening pain should be routed to the oncology team.

## Nausea, vomiting, and diarrhea

Nausea and vomiting can lead to dehydration. Diarrhea can be medication-related or infection-related and may become urgent if severe, persistent, bloody, or accompanied by fever, dizziness, or inability to keep fluids down. The assistant can log severity and encourage care-team review.

## Neuropathy

Neuropathy can feel like tingling, numbness, burning, or pain in hands or feet. It is commonly tracked during taxane or platinum treatment. The assistant may ask the patient to log severity and functional impact, but must not recommend dose changes.

## Fatigue

Fatigue is common during treatment but can also relate to anemia, infection, sleep disruption, pain, stress, or other causes. Severe or sudden fatigue should be discussed with the care team, especially with fever, shortness of breath, chest pain, dizziness, or low CBC values.

## Shortness of breath and chest pain

Shortness of breath, chest pain, fainting, or severe sudden symptoms should be escalated. The assistant should direct the patient to contact their oncology team or local emergency services.

## Breast wound, redness, swelling, or discharge

New wound changes, redness, warmth, swelling, pus-like drainage, bloody discharge, or worsening breast pain should be logged and reviewed by the care team. The assistant must not diagnose infection, recurrence, or metastasis.
