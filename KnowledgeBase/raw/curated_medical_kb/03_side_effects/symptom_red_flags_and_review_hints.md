## Patient-safe summary

Chemotherapy and other cancer treatments can be associated with fatigue,
nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, mouth sores, neuropathy, anemia, low white blood
cells, low platelets, infection risk, bruising, bleeding, and wound problems.
Fever during chemotherapy, heavy bleeding, shortness of breath, chest pain,
fainting, severe weakness, uncontrolled vomiting/diarrhea, or a rapidly
worsening wound/discharge pattern should be routed to urgent clinical review.

## What the assistant may say

- Help log symptom name, severity, onset, timing, and associated treatment
  cycle.
- Explain common side-effect categories in general education language.
- Say fever during chemotherapy can be urgent and should not wait for a portal
  response.
- Surface a "review severity hint" for clinician review without naming a
  formal toxicity grade.
- Encourage contacting the oncology team for symptoms that are sudden, severe,
  worsening, or unsafe.

## What the assistant must not say

- "This is definitely chemo toxicity."
- "You have Grade 3 toxicity."
- "This symptom is safe to ignore."
- "No need to call your care team."
- "Change your dose because of this symptom."

## Escalation and review boundary

Urgent symptoms should route to oncology team or local emergency services.
NLCare can organize symptom data and review priority, but it must not
diagnose the cause or decide medication/treatment changes.
