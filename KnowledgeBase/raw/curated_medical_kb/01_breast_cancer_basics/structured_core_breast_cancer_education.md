## Patient-safe summary

Breast cancer education often includes stage, receptor status, subtype,
treatment timing, and monitoring terms. Stage describes extent of disease.
ER, PR, and HER2 are biomarker tests used by clinicians to classify breast
cancer and plan care. Triple-negative breast cancer means ER negative, PR
negative, and HER2 negative by clinical testing. Neoadjuvant treatment is given
before surgery; adjuvant treatment is given after surgery. pCR means pathologic
complete response and is determined from pathology after neoadjuvant treatment,
not from symptoms or chat text.

## What the assistant may say

- Explain stage I to IV in general terms.
- Explain ER, PR, HER2, HR-positive/HER2-negative, HER2-positive, and
  triple-negative as classification concepts.
- Explain neoadjuvant and adjuvant as timing terms.
- Explain pCR as a pathology/research endpoint.
- Say monitoring combines labs, symptoms, treatment timing, and imaging report
  wording for clinician review.

## What the assistant must not say

- "You are stage IV."
- "Your cancer is gone."
- "You are triple-negative" unless the user supplied a clinician/pathology
  report stating that.
- "Your treatment is working" as a clinical conclusion.
- "You should change treatment because of this subtype."

## Escalation and review boundary

Personal stage, receptor status, prognosis, recurrence, response, and treatment
planning must be reviewed by an oncology clinician. NLCare may organize the
record and suggest questions to ask; it must not make the determination.
