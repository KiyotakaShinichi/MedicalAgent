# Breast Cancer Basics and Monitoring Terms

## Purpose

This document supports general education about breast cancer monitoring. It is not a diagnostic reference and must not be used to infer a patient's stage, subtype, prognosis, or treatment plan without clinician review.

## Breast cancer overview

Breast cancer is commonly described by tumor features, stage, receptor status, pathology findings, and treatment goals. Treatment can involve combinations of surgery, radiation therapy, chemotherapy, endocrine therapy, targeted therapy, and immunotherapy. Treatment selection depends on clinical and pathologic factors such as receptor status, tumor extent, lymph node involvement, prior treatment, and the clinician's treatment intent.

## Stage I to IV

Stage describes the extent of disease. Early-stage disease generally refers to cancer confined to the breast or nearby lymph nodes. Stage IV refers to metastatic disease, meaning cancer has spread to distant organs. OncoTrack must not assign or change a stage from patient-entered text. It may say that staging requires clinician interpretation of pathology and imaging.

## ER, PR, HER2

ER and PR refer to estrogen and progesterone receptor testing. HER2 refers to human epidermal growth factor receptor 2. These markers help clinicians classify breast cancer and select treatments. HR-positive/HER2-negative, HER2-positive, and triple-negative breast cancer are common subtype groupings. Triple-negative means ER-negative, PR-negative, and HER2-negative by clinical testing.

## Neoadjuvant and adjuvant treatment

Neoadjuvant treatment is given before surgery, often to shrink tumor burden or assess response. Adjuvant treatment is given after surgery to reduce recurrence risk. The assistant may explain these terms but must not decide whether a patient should receive either approach.

## pCR

pCR means pathologic complete response. In research and clinical contexts, it generally refers to no residual invasive cancer found in the surgical specimen after neoadjuvant treatment. pCR is determined by pathology, not by symptoms or a chatbot. OncoTrack can describe pCR as an engineering label or research endpoint, not as a patient-facing diagnosis.

## Recurrence and progression

Recurrence means cancer returns after treatment. Progression means disease worsens or grows. Imaging reports, pathology, symptoms, and clinician assessment are needed to interpret these terms. The assistant must not confirm recurrence or progression.

## Why monitoring uses CBC, symptoms, imaging, and treatment timeline

Treatment monitoring often reviews blood count trends, symptoms, treatment cycle timing, medications, and imaging report summaries together. CBC values can show treatment-related blood count changes. Symptom reports can surface side effects or urgent red flags. Imaging report text can track size and response language over time. These signals support clinician review but do not replace medical judgment.
