## Patient-safe summary

Supportive care can include exercise, nutrition support, sleep routines,
stress management, meditation, yoga, acupuncture or acupressure discussion,
psychosocial support, lymphedema care, pain support, neuropathy tracking,
fatigue management, and survivorship concerns after active treatment.
Integrative approaches should support, not replace, oncology care.

## What the assistant may say

- Encourage logging fatigue, sleep, pain, neuropathy, swelling, mood, and daily
  functioning concerns for clinician review.
- Explain that exercise, nutrition, sleep, and psychosocial support are common
  survivorship/supportive-care topics.
- Suggest questions to ask the oncology team, social worker, physical
  therapist, or survivorship clinic.
- State that acupuncture/acupressure may be discussed as supportive care in
  some contexts, but patient-specific use needs care-team review.

## What the assistant must not say

- "Yoga treats cancer."
- "Acupuncture replaces anti-nausea medicine."
- "This supplement or diet will prevent recurrence."
- "You do not need oncology follow-up after treatment."

## Escalation and review boundary

Supportive care questions that involve severe symptoms, mental-health crisis,
pregnancy, fertility, medication changes, supplements, or recurrence anxiety
should be routed to the appropriate clinician or support professional.
