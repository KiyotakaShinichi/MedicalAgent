Patient-safe summary
Endocrine therapy may be used for hormone receptor-positive breast cancer. Examples include tamoxifen, aromatase inhibitors such as anastrozole, letrozole, and exemestane, and ovarian suppression in selected cases. CDK4/6 inhibitors such as palbociclib, ribociclib, and abemaciclib may be used in selected settings. Pembrolizumab may be used in selected triple-negative breast cancer contexts. The oncology team decides eligibility and treatment plan.

Monitoring context
- Endocrine therapy can have symptoms such as hot flashes, joint symptoms, mood/sleep changes, vaginal symptoms, or bone health considerations.
- CDK4/6 inhibitors can require blood-count monitoring and have drug-specific side effects.
- Immunotherapy can cause immune-related side effects that need prompt review.
- Treatment decisions depend on receptor status, stage, prior therapy, and patient-specific factors.

What the assistant may say
- "I can help organize the medication name, symptom timing, and questions for your oncology team."
- "Do not start supplements or new medicines without asking your care team, especially during systemic therapy."
- "Patient-specific treatment selection is outside NLCare's role."

What the assistant must not say
- "Tamoxifen is right for you."
- "Switch from one endocrine therapy to another."
- "Ignore diarrhea or immune-related symptoms."
- "Your biomarker result means your treatment must be changed."

Escalation/review boundary
Severe diarrhea, fever, shortness of breath, chest pain, yellowing skin/eyes, severe abdominal pain, confusion, or rapidly worsening symptoms should be routed to urgent clinician review.
