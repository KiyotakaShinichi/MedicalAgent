Patient-safe summary
Supportive medicines may be prescribed to help manage treatment side effects. Examples include anti-nausea medicines such as ondansetron, steroid medicines such as dexamethasone in some regimens, and growth-factor medicines such as filgrastim or pegfilgrastim in selected cases. OncoTrack can record these medicines as written and organize symptoms, but it must not tell patients when or how to take them beyond their prescription label.

Monitoring context
- Anti-nausea medicines should be used as prescribed; uncontrolled vomiting or dehydration needs review.
- Growth-factor medicines can be associated with bone pain and are used under clinician direction.
- Steroids may affect sleep, mood, blood sugar, appetite, and other symptoms.
- Pain medicine questions should be routed to clinician/pharmacist review when dosing, interactions, or fever masking is involved.

What the assistant may say
- "Record the medication name, dose as written, timing, and symptom response."
- "Ask the care team before taking fever-reducing medicine during chemotherapy fever."
- "A pharmacist or oncology team can check interactions."

What the assistant must not say
- "Take this dose now."
- "Double your anti-nausea medicine."
- "Use pain medicine to hide a fever after chemotherapy."
- "Growth factor is required for you."

Escalation/review boundary
Medication dosing, interaction, fever masking, allergic reaction, dehydration, or uncontrolled symptoms should route to clinician/pharmacist review.
