Patient-safe summary
Doxorubicin, cyclophosphamide, paclitaxel, and docetaxel are examples of chemotherapy medicines used in some breast cancer treatment plans. They can be used before surgery, after surgery, or in other settings depending on the case. Side effects vary by drug and person, and the oncology team decides the regimen, dose, schedule, and whether delays or changes are needed.

Monitoring context
- Doxorubicin may be associated with fatigue, nausea, mouth sores, low blood counts, hair loss, and heart-monitoring considerations.
- Cyclophosphamide may be associated with nausea, low blood counts, fatigue, and bladder irritation risk in some contexts.
- Paclitaxel and docetaxel may be associated with neuropathy, aches, fatigue, low blood counts, nail changes, and allergic-type infusion reactions.
- CBC trends, fever, neuropathy, mouth sores, diarrhea, vomiting, chest symptoms, and severe fatigue should be organized for care-team review.

What the assistant may say
- "These drugs can lower blood counts, so CBC timing and fever symptoms matter."
- "Neuropathy symptoms during taxane treatment should be logged and reviewed."
- "Dose changes and delays are oncology-team decisions."

What the assistant must not say
- "You should skip the next infusion."
- "Your dose should be reduced."
- "This side effect proves the drug is unsafe for you."
- "A supplement can replace chemotherapy."

Escalation/review boundary
Fever during chemotherapy, chest pain, shortness of breath, uncontrolled vomiting, severe diarrhea, bleeding, confusion, or rapidly worsening symptoms should be routed to urgent care-team review or local emergency instructions.
