# Breast Cancer Treatment and Supportive Medication Reference

## Purpose

This reference supports general education about common breast cancer treatment and supportive medication classes. It does not recommend a regimen, dose, schedule, start/stop decision, or substitution. Medication decisions belong to the oncology care team.

## Chemotherapy

### Doxorubicin

Class: anthracycline chemotherapy.
Common use: may be part of breast cancer chemotherapy regimens.
Common side effects: fatigue, nausea, mouth sores, hair loss, low blood counts.
Serious side effects: heart toxicity risk, infection risk from low white blood cells, severe mucositis.
Monitoring needed: CBC trends and clinician-directed cardiac monitoring when appropriate.
Assistant may say: "Doxorubicin can lower blood counts and cause side effects that should be tracked."
Assistant must not say: "Your doxorubicin dose should change."

### Cyclophosphamide

Class: alkylating chemotherapy.
Common use: often paired with other chemotherapy drugs in breast cancer regimens.
Common side effects: nausea, fatigue, low blood counts, bladder irritation risk.
Monitoring needed: CBC trends, hydration instructions from the care team, urinary symptoms.

### Paclitaxel and docetaxel

Class: taxane chemotherapy.
Common use: frequently used in breast cancer treatment.
Common side effects: neuropathy, fatigue, low blood counts, hair loss, nail changes, aches.
Serious side effects: allergic or infusion reactions, infection risk, severe neuropathy.
Monitoring needed: symptoms such as tingling/numbness, CBC trends, infusion reaction history.

### Carboplatin

Class: platinum chemotherapy.
Common use: may be used in selected breast cancer settings, including some triple-negative regimens.
Common side effects: low blood counts, nausea, fatigue.
Monitoring needed: CBC, kidney function per clinician, symptoms.

### Capecitabine and gemcitabine

Class: antimetabolite chemotherapy.
Common use: may be used in selected breast cancer settings.
Common side effects: fatigue, nausea, diarrhea, low blood counts; capecitabine can cause hand-foot syndrome.
Monitoring needed: CBC, diarrhea severity, skin changes, dehydration risk.

## HER2-targeted therapy

### Trastuzumab and pertuzumab

Class: HER2-targeted monoclonal antibodies.
Common use: HER2-positive breast cancer.
Common side effects: diarrhea, fatigue, infusion reactions.
Serious side effects: heart function changes in some patients.
Monitoring needed: clinician-directed cardiac monitoring and symptom reporting.

### T-DM1 and trastuzumab deruxtecan

Class: antibody-drug conjugates.
Common use: HER2-positive breast cancer in selected settings.
Common side effects: fatigue, nausea, low blood counts, liver enzyme changes.
Serious side effects: lung inflammation/pneumonitis risk is especially important for trastuzumab deruxtecan.
Monitoring needed: new cough, shortness of breath, fever, CBC and liver tests per clinician.

## Endocrine therapy

### Tamoxifen

Class: selective estrogen receptor modulator.
Common use: hormone receptor positive breast cancer.
Common side effects: hot flashes, vaginal symptoms, mood changes.
Serious side effects: blood clot risk and uterine cancer risk in some groups.
Monitoring needed: new leg swelling, chest pain, abnormal bleeding, clinician follow-up.

### Aromatase inhibitors

Examples: anastrozole, letrozole, exemestane.
Common use: hormone receptor positive breast cancer, often in postmenopausal settings or with ovarian suppression.
Common side effects: joint pain, hot flashes, bone density loss.
Monitoring needed: bone health and symptom review.

### Ovarian suppression

Purpose: lowers ovarian estrogen production in selected premenopausal patients.
Monitoring needed: menopausal symptoms, bone health, clinician review.

## CDK4/6 inhibitors

Examples: palbociclib, ribociclib, abemaciclib.
Common use: hormone receptor positive, HER2-negative breast cancer in selected settings.
Common side effects: fatigue, low blood counts, diarrhea, nausea.
Serious side effects: infection risk, liver test changes, QT interval concerns with ribociclib.
Monitoring needed: CBC and labs per clinician; diarrhea severity for abemaciclib.

## Immunotherapy

### Pembrolizumab

Class: immune checkpoint inhibitor.
Common use: selected triple-negative breast cancer settings.
Common side effects: fatigue, rash, diarrhea, endocrine changes.
Serious side effects: immune-related inflammation affecting lungs, liver, colon, endocrine glands, kidneys, or other organs.
Monitoring needed: new diarrhea, cough, shortness of breath, severe fatigue, jaundice, severe headache, or unusual symptoms.

## Supportive medications

### Ondansetron and anti-nausea medicines

Purpose: help manage nausea and vomiting.
Assistant may say: "If nausea is present, log severity and ask the care team about anti-nausea options."
Assistant must not prescribe or change dose.

### Filgrastim and pegfilgrastim

Class: granulocyte colony-stimulating factors.
Purpose: support white blood cell recovery in selected patients.
Common side effects: bone pain, injection site discomfort.
Assistant may say: "Growth factor medicines may be used by clinicians to support blood counts in selected cases."
Assistant must not advise starting or skipping growth factor treatment.
