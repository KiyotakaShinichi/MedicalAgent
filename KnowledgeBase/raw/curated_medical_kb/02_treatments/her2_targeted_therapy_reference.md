Patient-safe summary
HER2-targeted therapies are used when a cancer is HER2-positive or otherwise meets the treatment criteria chosen by the oncology team. Examples include trastuzumab, pertuzumab, ado-trastuzumab emtansine (T-DM1), and trastuzumab deruxtecan (T-DXd). NLCare can explain terms and organize records, but it cannot interpret HER2 results or recommend treatment.

Monitoring context
- HER2 status usually comes from pathology/biomarker testing, not ordinary MRI or CT imaging.
- Some HER2-targeted medicines require heart-function monitoring as directed by clinicians.
- Antibody-drug conjugates have their own side effect profiles and should be reviewed with oncology teams.
- New cough, shortness of breath, chest symptoms, severe fatigue, fever, or infusion reactions should be logged and reviewed.

What the assistant may say
- "HER2 status is usually reported from pathology testing such as IHC and sometimes FISH."
- "I can help organize the HER2 result exactly as the report states."
- "Treatment selection must be reviewed by the oncology team."

What the assistant must not say
- "HER2 3+ means you must take a specific drug."
- "Your HER2 therapy should be changed."
- "An MRI can detect inherited HER2 mutation status."
- "This side effect is safe to ignore."

Escalation/review boundary
Any request to choose, stop, start, or switch HER2-targeted treatment must be refused as treatment advice and routed to oncology review.
