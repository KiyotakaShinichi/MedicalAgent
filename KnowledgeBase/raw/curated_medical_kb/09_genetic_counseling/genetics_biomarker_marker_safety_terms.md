## Patient-safe summary

Genetic counseling readiness organizes family history, germline/somatic test
records, variants, and patient questions for review. BRCA1, BRCA2, PALB2,
TP53, PTEN, CHEK2, and ATM can appear on hereditary breast cancer or multigene
panels. Germline testing looks for inherited variants, often from blood or
saliva. Somatic or tumor sequencing looks at tumor-specific changes. A VUS is a
variant of uncertain significance and should not be treated like a confirmed
pathogenic variant. Biomarkers such as ER, PR, HER2, and Ki-67 come from
pathology/biomarker testing. Tumor markers such as CA 15-3, CA 27.29, and CEA
are context-dependent blood tests and cannot diagnose recurrence by themselves.

## What the assistant may say

- Explain germline vs somatic testing in general terms.
- Explain that VUS means uncertain significance.
- Organize genetic-test, biomarker, and tumor-marker records for review.
- Suggest questions to ask a genetics-trained clinician, genetic counselor,
  oncology clinician, or pharmacist.
- Say tumor marker trends should be reviewed with symptoms, imaging, labs, and
  clinician context.

## What the assistant must not say

- "You have BRCA."
- "Your relatives will get cancer."
- "A VUS is positive."
- "HER2 3+ means you should change chemo."
- "CA 15-3 proves recurrence."
- "No genetic counselor is needed."

## Escalation and review boundary

Inherited-risk interpretation, family testing, VUS interpretation, biomarker
treatment implications, and tumor-marker trends require clinician or genetic
counselor review. NLCare organizes records and education only.
