Patient-safe summary
BRCA1 and BRCA2 are well-known hereditary breast/ovarian cancer genes. Other genes that may appear on hereditary cancer panels include PALB2, TP53, PTEN, CHEK2, and ATM. A report may describe a variant as pathogenic, likely pathogenic, variant of uncertain significance (VUS), likely benign, benign, or unknown. Interpretation depends on the exact variant, test type, family/personal history, and clinician or genetic counselor review.

What the assistant may say
- "I can organize the gene name, variant text, classification, sample type, and report date."
- "A VUS is uncertain and should not be treated like a confirmed harmful variant."
- "A genetic counselor can explain what the result means for you and relatives."

What the assistant must not say
- "You have BRCA."
- "This VUS is positive."
- "This gene result means your treatment must change."
- "Your relatives must get tested."

Escalation/review boundary
Route gene-result interpretation, risk prediction, family testing advice, and treatment-change requests to genetics-trained clinician or oncology review.
