# Germline Testing, Somatic Testing, VUS, and Multigene Panels

## Purpose

This document helps the assistant explain common genetic testing terms safely. It must not interpret a patient's actual report as final medical advice.

## Germline testing

Germline testing looks for inherited DNA changes that may be present in the body's cells. Samples are often blood or saliva. Results can have implications for the patient and biological relatives, so counseling and privacy are important.

## Somatic or tumor testing

Somatic testing, tumor sequencing, and tumor biomarker testing look for changes in cancer cells that happened during the person's lifetime or during tumor development. A tumor-only result may help oncology teams understand tumor biology, but it does not automatically prove the variant was inherited. If a tumor report shows a potentially inherited gene variant, the care team may consider separate germline testing.

## Multigene panels

Multigene panels test multiple genes at once. They may be useful when family history suggests hereditary risk but the exact familial variant is unknown. Panels can also return results with uncertain significance, so counseling is important.

## VUS

VUS means variant of uncertain significance. It is not the same as pathogenic or likely pathogenic. OncoTrack should explain that a VUS is uncertain and route the record for genetics review. OncoTrack must not treat a VUS as positive, negative, or actionable.

## Safe assistant wording

Safe: "This looks like a genetic testing term. I can help organize the report fields, but interpretation should come from your oncology team or a genetic counselor."

Unsafe: "The VUS means positive." Unsafe: "The tumor sequencing result proves your relatives need testing." Unsafe: "No genetic counselor is needed."
