# Breast Cancer Biomarkers and Tumor Marker Safety

## Purpose

This document supports safe education about biomarker/pathology results and tumor marker lab trends. It does not diagnose recurrence, decide treatment, or interpret a report without clinician review.

## Biomarkers

Breast cancer cells are commonly tested for estrogen receptor, progesterone receptor, and HER2. Ki-67 may also be reported. These tests are typically performed on cancer cells from biopsy or surgery pathology, not by looking at ordinary CT, MRI, or ultrasound scans.

ER, PR, HER2, and Ki-67 help clinicians understand tumor biology and plan care. NLCare may organize the status values if they are present in the report, but it must not infer a missing result or recommend treatment based on a biomarker.

## IHC and FISH

HER2 may be reported by immunohistochemistry, often written as IHC 0, 1+, 2+, or 3+. Some equivocal HER2 results may have additional FISH or ISH testing for gene amplification. NLCare can store the exact wording but must not convert or reinterpret it beyond the report text unless the user has already supplied the clinician's interpretation.

## Tumor markers

CA 15-3, CA 27.29, and CEA are blood tumor markers that may be followed in some breast cancer contexts. They are context-dependent and should not be treated as a standalone diagnosis, screening test, or proof that cancer has returned. Trends should be reviewed with symptoms, imaging, labs, and the oncology team's assessment.

## Safe assistant wording

Safe: "A high tumor marker is a reason to review the full context with your oncology team. It cannot diagnose recurrence by itself."

Safe: "HER2 3+ is a biomarker result that your oncology team uses in treatment planning. I can organize the record but cannot advise changing treatment."

Unsafe: "Your tumor marker proves cancer is back." Unsafe: "HER2 3+ means you should change chemo." Unsafe: "Ki-67 means your cancer is definitely aggressive."
