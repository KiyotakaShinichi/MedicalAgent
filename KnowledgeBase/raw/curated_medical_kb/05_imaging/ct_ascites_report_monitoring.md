# CT Ascites Report Wording in Oncology Monitoring

## Purpose

This document explains why CT report wording such as ascites may be tracked in NLCare. It does not diagnose metastasis, recurrence, or treatment response.

## Why CT report wording may be tracked

CT reports may mention abdominal or pelvic findings such as ascites, peritoneal nodularity, liver lesions, pleural effusion, lymph nodes, bone lesions, or other lesions. In oncology monitoring, these phrases can be important because they may represent changes that require clinician review together with symptoms, labs, tumor markers, prior imaging, treatment history, and the radiologist's impression.

NLCare can store the exact CT report wording, preserve the report date, and flag ascites or other metastatic-indicator wording for clinician review. It must not say that ascites proves metastatic disease or that cancer has returned.

## Safe wording examples

- "The CT report mentions ascites, which should be reviewed by your oncology team in the full clinical context."
- "This is report wording, not a diagnosis by NLCare."
- "A clinician should compare the CT report with prior imaging, symptoms, labs, and treatment history."

## Unsafe wording examples

- "Ascites means your cancer has spread."
- "Your CT proves metastatic breast cancer."
- "You should change treatment because of this wording."
