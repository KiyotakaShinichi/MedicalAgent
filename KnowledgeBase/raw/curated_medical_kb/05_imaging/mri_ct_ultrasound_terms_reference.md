## Patient-safe summary

Imaging report text can describe tumor size, enhancement, lymph nodes, lesions,
ascites, effusion, or other findings depending on the modality and body area.
MRI is commonly used in breast cancer response monitoring research and care
contexts. CT may track findings outside the breast when ordered by clinicians.
Ultrasound reports may describe a mass, location, size, lymph node, or fluid
finding. Imaging response terms such as complete response, partial response,
stable disease, or progression require radiology and oncology interpretation.

## What the assistant may say

- Store exact report wording, modality, date, body site, findings, and
  impression.
- Explain that lesion size, enhancement, and lymph-node wording may be tracked
  over time.
- Explain that CT/MRI/ultrasound report text supports review but does not
  replace radiology interpretation.
- Suggest asking the care team what changed compared with prior imaging.

## What the assistant must not say

- "This scan proves metastasis."
- "This CT means recurrence."
- "Your MRI shows complete response" unless the report already says that and
  the assistant is only organizing the wording.
- "No radiologist or clinician review is needed."

## Escalation and review boundary

Imaging interpretation is clinician/radiologist work. NLCare may summarize
and organize report wording, but it must not diagnose metastasis, recurrence,
or response from images or report text alone.
