## Patient-safe summary

Supplements and natural products can interact with cancer therapy, surgery,
anesthesia, anticoagulants, endocrine therapy, targeted therapy, immunotherapy,
or supportive medicines. Evidence that supplements cure cancer is insufficient.
Patients should tell the oncology team or oncology pharmacist before starting,
stopping, or combining supplements during treatment.

## Product-specific safety notes

- Turmeric/curcumin: not a replacement for chemotherapy; review bleeding and
  interaction concerns with the care team.
- Green tea extract: concentrated extracts are different from beverage use and
  may have liver or interaction concerns.
- St. Johns wort: high interaction concern because it can affect drug
  metabolism pathways and may reduce medication effects.
- Ginger: may be discussed for nausea support, but supplement use should be
  reviewed, especially with bleeding risk or procedures.
- Garlic and ginkgo: may raise bleeding or medication-interaction concerns in
  some contexts.
- Ashwagandha: not cancer treatment; review liver, immune, sedation, and
  interaction concerns.
- Cannabis/CBD: legality and safety vary; may interact with medicines and
  affect sedation/cognition.
- High-dose antioxidants or vitamin C: not proven replacements for cancer
  treatment and may raise treatment-interaction concerns.
- Vitamin D, iron, B12/folate, omega-3, probiotics: clinician-directed
  replacement is different from self-directed high-dose use; review labs,
  immune status, bleeding risk, and medication context.

## What the assistant may say

- "Please ask your oncology team or oncology pharmacist before using this."
- "Supplements are not proven replacements for cancer treatment."
- "I can log this supplement question for clinician review."
- "Interaction risk depends on treatment, dose, surgery/procedure timing, labs,
  and other medications."

## What the assistant must not say

- "This supplement is safe with chemo."
- "Use turmeric instead of chemotherapy."
- "Antioxidants protect you from treatment side effects."
- "CBD is safe for your regimen."
- "No need to tell your doctor."

## Escalation and review boundary

Any patient-specific supplement, herb, vitamin, cannabis/CBD, or interaction
question during active treatment should route to oncology/pharmacist review.
