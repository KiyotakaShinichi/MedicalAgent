# Supplements, Vitamins, Herbs, and Natural Remedy Interaction Safety

## Purpose

This document supports safe education about supplements during cancer treatment. It must not recommend supplements as cancer treatment. It must not suggest replacing chemotherapy, radiation, surgery, endocrine therapy, targeted therapy, immunotherapy, or clinician-prescribed supportive medications.

## Core safety message

Evidence for herbal supplements as cancer treatment is limited. Some supplements can interact with cancer drugs, surgery, anesthesia, blood thinners, hormone therapy, immunotherapy, or supportive medications. Patients should tell the oncology team or oncology pharmacist about every supplement, vitamin, herbal product, tea extract, cannabis/CBD product, or high-dose nutrient they use or are considering.

## Antioxidants during chemotherapy or radiation

High-dose antioxidant supplements may be risky during some chemotherapy or radiation contexts because they could theoretically interfere with treatment mechanisms or interact with therapy. The assistant should say: ask the oncology team before taking antioxidant supplements during active treatment.

## Turmeric and curcumin

Turmeric and curcumin are commonly discussed as natural products. They are not proven replacements for cancer treatment. They may have interaction concerns, including bleeding risk or drug metabolism concerns depending on formulation and patient context. The assistant must refuse "turmeric instead of chemotherapy" requests.

## Green tea extract

Green tea beverage and concentrated green tea extract are not the same. Concentrated extracts may have liver toxicity or interaction concerns. The assistant should not recommend green tea extract dosing.

## Ginger

Ginger may be discussed for nausea support, but supplement doses and interactions should be reviewed by the care team. The assistant can suggest logging nausea severity and asking about nausea management.

## Garlic, ginkgo, and bleeding-risk supplements

Garlic supplements and ginkgo may raise bleeding concerns or interact with medicines in some patients. This matters around surgery, low platelets, anticoagulants, or procedures. The assistant should route bleeding symptoms or supplement questions to clinician review.

## St. Johns wort

St. Johns wort can interact with many medicines through drug metabolism pathways. It should not be started during cancer treatment without oncology/pharmacist review.

## Ashwagandha

Ashwagandha is not a cancer treatment. It may have safety concerns, liver concerns, immune effects, sedation, or interactions depending on context. The assistant should not recommend it during treatment without clinician review.

## Cannabis and CBD

Cannabis and CBD legality and safety vary by location and patient context. They may interact with medicines and affect sedation or cognition. The assistant may recommend discussing with the oncology team, not self-starting during treatment.

## Probiotics

Probiotics may not be appropriate for every patient, especially during immunosuppression or severe neutropenia. The assistant should encourage oncology review before use during active chemotherapy.

## Vitamin D, calcium, iron, B12, folate, omega-3

Some vitamins or minerals may be prescribed or recommended when deficiency or bone health issues are present. Self-directed high-dose use is different from clinician-directed replacement. Iron, B12, folate, vitamin D, calcium, and omega-3 should be discussed with the care team, especially if labs, kidney function, bleeding risk, or treatment interactions are relevant.

## High-dose vitamin C

High-dose vitamin C is not a proven replacement for cancer treatment and may have safety or interaction concerns. The assistant must not recommend it as cancer therapy.

## Safe answer pattern

1. Acknowledge the question.
2. State that supplements are not proven replacements for cancer treatment.
3. Mention interaction/safety concerns.
4. Recommend oncology team or oncology pharmacist review before use.
5. Offer to log the supplement question for clinician review.
